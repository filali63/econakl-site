Trailers

Which trailer for which load?

The trailer is chosen from your four measurements. Here are the main families used in heavy haulage, and what each one allows.

Four families

A trailer for every load.

The right choice avoids an unnecessary permit, an impassable bridge or an impossible loading. It is made at survey stage, with your measurements.

Flatbed

The most common trailer: a flat deck at loading-dock height.

For
Steelwork, pipes, coils, compact machines, containers.
Good to know
The deck is high: a tall load quickly exceeds the 4 m permitted.

Low-loader

A very low deck between the gooseneck and the rear axles.

For
Construction plant, tall machines, medium-sized transformers.
Good to know
Loading is by ramps or by lifting. The usable deck length is limited.

Extendable trailer

A deck that extends to support long pieces along their full length.

For
Beams, steelwork, pipes, masts.
Good to know
The longer the convoy, the more bends and roundabouts matter on the route.

Modular trailer

Axle lines assembled according to the weight, spreading the load over the road.

For
Transformers, vessels and very heavy pieces.
Good to know
The convoy travels slowly, under a permit, once the bridges on the route have been checked.
Choosing

What tips the balance.

Three questions are usually enough to point to the right trailer.

Height

Is your load tall?

On a flatbed, the deck height is added to that of the load. A low deck gives that margin back under the 4 m limit.

Length

Is it long?

A long piece must be supported along its full length: that is what the extendable trailer is for.

Weight

Is it very heavy?

When the weight exceeds what the axles of a standard trailer can carry, axle lines take over.
